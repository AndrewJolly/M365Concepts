Scope & interpretation

Microsoft 365 retention labelling, with adjacent policies and controls shown where they affect the lifecycle. This is a capability map, not a deployment script. It does not imply that every option works with every workload or record type. It does not cover third-party records repositories, on-premises retention or every API and licensing permutation.

Business classification, retention treatment and sensitivity protection are separate design decisions. Examples in guided paths are illustrative, not prescribed retention periods. Archive storage is also distinct from transfer to a collecting archive. Validate feature availability and restrictions for the target tenant before deployment.

    Capability notes & source links

    Retention authority

    Translate the approved schedule into business classes, triggers, periods, outcomes and accountable owners. Maintain traceability to the relevant authority.

    Limits: Design responsibility: configuring Purview does not determine the legal validity of a disposal decision.

    Sources: File plan

    File plan & label register

    File plan describes labels using reference IDs, business functions, categories and authority citations; supports CSV import and export.

    Limits: Descriptors organise the plan; they do not themselves classify documents or map a SharePoint term to a label.

    Sources: File plan

    Documents & list items

    Files are governed in their underlying storage. Supported SharePoint list items can use labels even though retention policies do not cover list items.

    Limits: Standard list-item labels do not automatically retain attachments. Record labels can confer retention on unlabelled attachments.

    Sources: SharePoint and OneDrive retention

    Email & mailbox content

    Retention processing uses mailbox item dates and the Managed Folder Assistant. Retained deleted content uses Recoverable Items.

    Limits: Purview labels differ from Exchange MRM tags. Group mailbox label support differs from ordinary mailboxes.

    Sources: Exchange retention, Retention settings

    Messages & modern content

    Teams chats, channel messages and Copilot interactions use workload retention policies. Teams files use SharePoint or OneDrive.

    Limits: Loop and Copilot Pages support varies by storage and application; published standard labels are supported through the Loop app.

    Sources: Retention policies and labels, Publish and apply labels

    Business classification

    Design a controlled mapping from business metadata to retention classes. For query matching, map SharePoint fields to supported predefined refinable managed properties.

    Limits: Use the actual managed-property name, not its alias. Search indexing and schema propagation are dependencies.

    Sources: Auto-apply labels

    Business event source

    Identify the authoritative business date and the items belonging to the event. Arrange a manual procedure or an integration to create the Purview event.

    Limits: A populated Close Date column alone does not create an event. Integration and reconciliation are design responsibilities.

    Sources: Event-based retention

    Locations & policy scope

    Static scopes select locations; adaptive scopes use supported attributes. Scope determines where a policy operates.

    Limits: Location and item matching are different decisions. Check group-connected sites, channel sites and SharePoint Embedded separately.

    Sources: Adaptive scopes, Retention settings

    Retention label definition

    A label configures item-level treatment. Keep a durable name, useful user guidance and a documented mapping to the business class.

    Limits: Names cannot be renamed after creation. File plan changes must be managed alongside label settings.

    Sources: File plan

    Standard label

    Supports ordinary working content. It does not impose the full record restrictions.

    Limits: Users can normally edit; deletion can be blocked by a tenant setting. Retention and edit protection are separate controls.

    Sources: Records and restrictions

    Record declaration

    A record label restricts actions. SharePoint and OneDrive support locked and unlocked states; Exchange records follow locked-record behaviour.

    Limits: Unlocking permits edits, not user deletion. Locked-record property editing is a separate tenant setting.

    Sources: Records and restrictions, Declare records

    Regulatory record

    Blocks content and property edits and prevents removal of the applied label, including by a global administrator.

    Limits: Not supported by auto-apply label policies. Enable the creation option deliberately; a saved regulatory label’s period cannot be shortened.

    Sources: Records and restrictions, Declare records

    Retention action modes

    Choose retain-only, retain then delete, delete-only, or classification without enforcement. Retention can be finite or indefinite.

    Limits: Delete-only does not preserve content until its deletion date. Record declaration is a separate design choice.

    Sources: Retention policies and labels, Retention settings

    Retention clock

    Select the supported starting point and duration. Modified-date starts apply to SharePoint and OneDrive files, not Exchange messages.

    Limits: Existing content can already be old enough to expire. Duration is calculated from the configured trigger, not policy deployment.

    Sources: Retention settings

    Baseline retention policy

    Apply broad retention separately from item labels. A baseline can preserve items that have no label.

    Limits: The policy does not apply a label. Its retention obligations can continue alongside a labelled item.

    Sources: Retention policies and labels

    Preservation Lock

    Prevents administrators from disabling, deleting or making a locked retention policy less restrictive. Supported label-policy scenarios have additional constraints.

    Limits: Irreversible policy-level control. It is distinct from locking an individual record or declaring a regulatory record; verify eligible configuration first.

    Sources: Preservation Lock

    Publish labels

    A publishing policy distributes selected labels to supported locations for use.

    Limits: Publishing alone does not label every item. Distribution is asynchronous; allow up to seven days.

    Sources: Publish and apply labels

    User application

    Users select a published label in the supported application. Outlook rules can also apply labels.

    Limits: User permissions and application support still apply. Training should explain when a label declares a record.

    Sources: Publish and apply labels

    Container defaults

    A published default labels eligible unlabelled items. SharePoint libraries can also apply a default to existing items.

    Limits: Defaults are inheritance rules, not container retention. Existing labels, moves and migrated files need separate testing; Outlook inheritance differs.

    Sources: Publish and apply labels

    Query-based auto-apply

    A service policy matches a search query and applies its selected label. Metadata can drive deterministic business-class matching.

    Limits: Does not replace an existing label. Rule or metadata changes do not automatically remove or reclassify previously labelled items.

    Sources: Auto-apply labels

    Sensitive information

    Use supported sensitive information types as an auto-apply condition.

    Limits: Exact Data Match and document fingerprinting are unsupported here. Exchange existing-item coverage and custom SIT backlogs have restrictions.

    Sources: Auto-apply labels

    Trainable classifiers

    Apply a label when content matches a supported classifier.

    Limits: Existing-item evaluation is limited to the last six months. Test accuracy and supported workloads.

    Sources: Auto-apply labels

    Cloud attachments

    A specialised auto-apply policy retains copies of files shared as cloud attachments through supported email and Teams scenarios.

    Limits: Sharing-based capture differs from applying a label to every live source document. Check policy prerequisites and version behaviour.

    Sources: Auto-apply labels

    Models, flows & APIs

    Published labels can be associated with a supported Syntex model. Power Automate can apply a label; Graph can set a driveItem retention label.

    Limits: Custom orchestration must handle permissions, existing labels, record restrictions, failures and evidence. These routes are not a universal auto-apply policy.

    Sources: Publish and apply labels, Power Automate at expiry, Microsoft Graph: setRetentionLabel

    Label applied to item

    The applied label determines that item’s label configuration. Sensitivity labels can coexist independently.

    Limits: No sensitivity-style priority ladder. Resolve overlapping classification rules before rollout.

    Sources: Retention policies and labels

    Event starts the clock

    Create an event matching the label’s event type and the relevant asset ID or query. Its date starts retention for matching labelled, indexed content. Until triggered, event-based retention continues to preserve the content.

    Limits: Without a narrowing query the event can affect all matching event-type content. Late-labelled items need another event; deleting an event does not undo it.

    Sources: Event-based retention

    Lock, unlock & versions

    Record versioning supports controlled updates while preserving record copies in the Preservation Hold library.

    Limits: Different from ordinary version history. Not available for Exchange or regulatory records; record state affects available actions.

    Sources: Record versioning

    Preservation Hold library

    SharePoint preserves content when required. A standard retention label alone does not create a preservation copy on every edit; retained deletion does.

    Limits: Record and policy behaviour differs. The library consumes site storage and is not an interactive records archive.

    Sources: SharePoint and OneDrive retention

    Recoverable Items

    Mailbox processing retains required deleted content in protected mailbox folders until it becomes eligible for removal.

    Limits: User-visible deletion is not proof of permanent deletion. Other holds and mailbox processing influence the outcome.

    Sources: Exchange retention

    Resolve retention & holds

    Normally, retain beats delete; the latest applicable retention end date controls preservation. A label’s delete action takes precedence over policy delete actions.

    Limits: Other holds still matter. Explicit policy targeting and then the shortest deletion period resolve remaining policy deletion conflicts. Priority cleanup is an exception.

    Sources: Retention policies and labels

    Microsoft 365 Archive

    Current documentation describes an optional archive action for eligible inactive SharePoint files while retention and holds continue.

    Limits: File-level archive excludes event-based retention. Verify tenant availability, E5 eligibility and billing; archiving is not disposal or archival-authority transfer.

    Sources: Retention settings, Archive compliance

    Period expires

    Finite retention leads to the configured end action: deletion, review, relabelling, flow or deactivation, subject to supported combinations.

    Limits: Indefinite retention has no scheduled expiry. Reaching a date does not prove that permanent deletion has occurred.

    Sources: Retention settings

    Disposition review

    Assigned reviewers approve, extend or relabel. Any authorised reviewer in a stage can act.

    Limits: Optional auto-approval can advance or dispose without manual action after a configured timeout; it must be an explicit governance decision.

    Sources: Disposition

    Deletion & approval

    Automatic deletion or final disposition approval initiates removal when the item is eligible.

    Limits: Other retention requirements and processing can delay deletion. An approval is not itself a deletion certificate.

    Sources: Disposition, Exchange retention

    Extend review

    Extending a pending disposition suspends review until the extended period ends, then restarts from the first stage.

    Limits: This is not a business event and does not edit the label’s retention duration for every item.

    Sources: Disposition

    Apply another label

    A configured expiry action or disposition decision can replace the label. The replacement’s own clock and rules apply.

    Limits: Regulatory records cannot be relabelled. A replacement does not necessarily start a fresh full period.

    Sources: Retention settings

    Deactivate retention settings

    A supported retain-only label can stop enforcing its retention settings after the period.

    Limits: The label remains. Record lock/unlock restrictions can remain; other policies or holds still apply.

    Sources: Retention settings

    Custom end-of-period flow

    A dedicated Power Automate trigger supports a custom procedure at expiry. Design retries and durable evidence for the business process.

    Limits: Requires a new label for this configuration. Use My flows, not Solutions; flow ownership and licensing need planning.

    Sources: Power Automate at expiry

    Disposition evidence

    Disposition provides review history and information about disposed records; CSV exports support evidence capture.

    Limits: Auditing must be enabled. Preserve required evidence under its own retention arrangement.

    Sources: Disposition

    Roles & reviewers

    Disposition Management permits review actions; content preview needs additional permissions. Seeing all reviews is a separate administrative configuration.

    Limits: Adding a reviewer does not grant the required roles. Test using actual reviewer accounts.

    Sources: Disposition

    Licensing & service limits

    Check entitlements for each feature and benefiting user, including records management, automatic application, events and advanced lifecycle actions.

    Limits: Avoid a single E3/E5 badge for the whole design. Service limits and add-ons differ; use current product guidance.

    Sources: Roles and licensing prerequisites, Retention service limits

    Deploy & monitor

    Pilot representative content; use supported simulation, policy status, item checks and audit evidence. Reconcile exceptions and unlabeled content.

    Limits: Design check: distinguish publishing delays, indexing delays, matching failures, existing-label conflicts and later lifecycle processing.

    Sources: Auto-apply labels, Retention service limits, Roles and licensing prerequisites

    Change & migration

    Maintain a change log for labels, mappings, scopes, events and business authorities. Validate dates and classifications after migration.

    Limits: Unpublishing is not a rollback of applied labels. Some settings are immutable; shortening periods can accelerate eligibility.

    Sources: File plan, Publish and apply labels, Retention settings

    Priority cleanup

    Dedicated cleanup can override specified retention and eDiscovery holds for eligible content, with workload-specific controls and approvals.

    Limits: Excluded for items declared records. Treat this as an exceptional process, not an ordinary retention expiry route.

    Sources: Priority cleanup for files, Priority cleanup for mail

    Adaptive Protection

    The DLM integration can automatically retain otherwise unlabelled content deleted by elevated-risk users for 120 days.

    Limits: System-managed protection, not a business file plan. Verify preview availability and integration settings.

    Sources: Adaptive Protection
