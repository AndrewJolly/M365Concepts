Interactive timeline · Microsoft Purview event-based retention

Date Closed to disposition — three-lane timeline

A time-based explanation of the customer’s event-driven retention approach. This view shows how a record can be created, classified and labelled before retention actually starts, and how Date Closed later becomes the trigger used by Purview events to calculate the retention expiry date and move the item toward disposition review.

What this timeline is trying to make obvious

The hardest concept in this design is that the item can already have the right label, but still not yet have a retention expiry date. The yellow waiting stage below highlights that gap between label applied and event processed.

Lane 1 — Business lifecycleWhat the user or business process does to the item.
Lane 2 — Purview lifecycleWhat the compliance platform does in response.
Lane 3 — Evidence & controlsWhat proves the configuration is working.
Sample dates5 Jan 2026 to 31 Mar 2033, illustrating a seven-year retention example.
Important: applying the event-based retention label does not by itself start the retention period. Retention begins when a matching Purview event processes the labelled item and calculates the expiry date from the event date.
Tip: click any step on the timeline. Related steps remain bright, and the detail panel will explain what happens, why it matters, the main dependency, and what evidence is available.

Use of this artifact: This version is deliberately timeline-centric so that stakeholders can see the difference between classifying and labelling an item and actually commencing retention. If you want, this can next be tailored with your customer’s exact terms, label names, managed property names, or even representative test IDs from the Test Plan.
